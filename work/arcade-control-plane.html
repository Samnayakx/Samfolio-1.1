    Work/Arcade Control Plane

    Case study ( 04 ) · Arcade.dev

    Turning Arcade from an infrastructure inventory into an agent deployment control plane.

    I led the redesign of Arcade.dev's dashboard into a deployment control plane. Median time to a first authorized tool call fell from 47 minutes to 8.

    Role
    Lead Product Designer
    Timeline
    Mar – Jun 2026, 14 weeks
    Team
    1 PM, 5 engineers, 1 DevRel, 1 designer
    Tools
    Figma, shadcn/ui, Cursor + Figma MCP, PostHog

    01Overview / Background

    Arcade wins when the first tool call works.

    Arcade is the auth and tool-calling layer for AI agents. New teams signed up, browsed the dashboard and left before their agent made a single authorized call.

    Over 14 weeks I led the redesign that turned a resource inventory into a control plane built around that first call.

    • Problem: 47 minutes to a first call

      Users assembled Arcade's runtime in their heads before anything ran.

    • Solution: four surfaces, one path

      Navigation, onboarding, Playground and Project Home, rebuilt around the first call.

    • Outcome: 8 minutes

      Median time to a first authorized call. Day-one activation rose from 22% to 61%.

    02Defining the Problem

    Users met the architecture before the value.

    The dashboard listed 18 destinations and about 8,000 tools. Before anything ran, a new user had to choose code, gateway or sandbox, then stitch docs, config and OAuth together across four places.

    So we asked: how might a new team reach a working, authorized call in one session, without leaving for the docs?

    03Understanding your Users

    AI engineers who quit at OAuth, not out of curiosity.

    We interviewed 12 AI engineers for 30 minutes each, shadowed three live setups and replayed 340 onboarding sessions in PostHog.

    12AI engineers interviewed, 30 minutes each
    340onboarding sessions replayed
    64%of new projects never finished OAuth

    • 1. Setup lives outside the product

      8 of 12 opened the docs in a second tab before their first click.

    • 2. Auth is the cliff

      Most stalled projects were missing a single scope.

    • 3. Failures are silent

      9 of 12 couldn't say why a call failed, so they stopped trying.

    “I had the agent working locally in ten minutes. Getting it permission to touch Linear took the afternoon.”

    AI engineer, Series A startup

    04Your Role and Responsibilities

    The only designer, from framing the problem to shipped UI.

    I led design end to end: research, information architecture, onboarding, AI UX and the coded prototype engineering built from. "We" below means me, a PM, five engineers and DevRel.

    Weekly workshops with engineering kept the design honest about how auth, scopes and the gateway really work.

    • Lead Product Designer

      Research, IA, onboarding, AI UX, prototyping and QA.

    • Team of 8

      1 PM, 5 engineers, 1 DevRel and me.

    • 14 weeks

      March to June 2026, shipped in two releases.

    05Scope and Constraints

    Build one path completely, and defer the rest.

    We ranked every request with MoSCoW. Anything that didn't shorten the path to the first call waited.

    • Success metric

      Median time to a first authorized tool call.

    • Guardrail

      No new drop-off at the auth step.

    • Won't, for now

      Users, policies, runs, audit and usage.

    06Ideation Steps (The Solution)

    Four surfaces, each shortening the path to the first call.

    We moved a new user from a resource inventory to a first authorized call in four steps.

    Rejected designsFour variations that failed in testing.

    We ran moderated sessions with five engineers on each lo-fi concept. These four failed, and each failure shaped what shipped.

    Feature 01Group by the job, not the object.

    Same features, regrouped into Build, Roll out and Observe. 18 destinations became 12.

    Feature 02Intent before infrastructure.

    Ask what they want done, recommend the fastest path, and keep expert paths one click away. Sandbox is the default: safe to test, promoted later.

    Feature 03A runtime simulator, not a chat box.

    Every run shows the same eight checks. When a tool needs access, the run pauses and asks in place, one click to authorize.

    Feature 04One page, four states.

    Home follows the team's progress from empty to active, and every blocker ships with its fix.

    07Outcomes and Takeaways

    A first authorized call in 8 minutes, not 47.

    Measured on new projects six weeks after the second release, against the quarter before.

    47→8minutes, median time to a first authorized call
    22→61%of new teams made an authorized call on day one
    −38%auth-related support tickets

    • Guardrail held

      Drop-off at the auth step fell from 64% to 29%.

    • 3.4× traces opened

      Users read the run instead of retrying blind.

    • Now the default

      The trace pattern now carries into Runs and Audit.

    TakeawayPick the moment that matters, and build the path to it.

    The biggest gain came from what we deferred, not what we added. The honest limit: teams with an existing gateway barely moved, and they are the next study.
