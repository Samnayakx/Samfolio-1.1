    02Problem

    One events app has to serve people who go and people who host.

    The brief was an events app in the spirit of Airbnb, designed for an agency in three months. Someone had to be able to find an event and get a ticket, and someone else to host their own.

    That is two products in one app: a guest who browses, registers and shows a ticket at the door, and a host who creates an event, approves guests, checks them in and gets paid. Both had to feel like one product, on iOS, in light and dark.

    03Hypothesis

    Nine flows are enough for the whole product.

    Cover the product with nine flows, from sign-in to payouts, in light and dark, so a three-month MVP stays consistent.

    If every task a guest or a host needs maps to one of nine flows, the MVP can stay small and still be complete.

    04Solution

    Find an event, get a ticket, host your own.

    Gatherly has two sides. A guest finds an event, registers, and shows a ticket at the door. A host creates the event, approves guests, checks them in and gets paid. Here is each side in motion, then the final screens in light and dark.

    Key flowsNine journeys cover the whole product.

    Nine flows take a person from first launch to payout: onboarding, discover, event and register, tickets and event day, messaging, three host flows, and profile. Each row shows the key screens of one flow.

    Interaction designEvery tap has a gesture, a transition and a reason.

    Four flows annotated for hand-off. Each tap target is ringed and numbered, and each arrow names the gesture and how the screen moves: pushes slide in from the right, sheets rise from the bottom, and buttons show a spinner before the success state. Click a board to read it at full size.

    05Learning

    The MVP shipped.

    Three months from brief to a shipped MVP: 64 screens in light and dark, nine flows, and interaction specs for every key tap.

    A few decisions held the product together. Price is always plain: free is free, paid is clear. Every registration has an explicit state, whether confirmed, pending approval or on the waitlist. Refund and cancellation rules are explained before someone needs them.
